# v2.4: v24-3d (the swoop, the bullet paths, a fight at a fort)

Branch `claude/v24-3d-93ee66`, from 017a215 (v2.3.1). No rules change, no outcome change: staging,
drawing and loading only.

## 1. The chug between the map and a battle (Alex: "the zoom in between the main map and battle map on 3d absolutely chugs")

### What it was (measured, not guessed)

`tools/study/swoop.gd` plays the real client on the land as Alex plays it (board on the land, battles on
the land, motion "all", rolling himself, Wide World) through several battles. It records every frame's
real length (`Time.get_ticks_usec`, the first thing each frame does) and, for the slow ones, what ran in
them: resources newly loaded from disk, nodes and resources made, pipelines compiled (by kind), draw
calls, process time, render CPU and GPU time (the screen's and the land's), and the board's overlay
draws, timed.

On this PC (RTX 3070, i7-10700KF, Vulkan Forward+, 1440x900), **render and GPU were 1-6 ms a frame.**
It wasn't the 3D, and it wasn't asset loading. **It was the board's 2D overlay** (`map_board.gd`
`_draw_overlay`, drawn through the land's camera by `projector`/`unprojector`):

| piece | cost per overlay draw | why |
|---|---|---|
| `_draw_region_labels` → `_bonus_spot` | **20-415 ms** while the camera moves | `_bonus_at` is cleared every 0.15 s while the zoom or the layout changes, and each search tries up to 2,673 spots per region, asking `_region_at` (an `unproject` through the 3D camera, 6 height lookups) three times a spot |
| the tokens' shapes (`Style.draw_shape` ×5, `draw_circle` ×5 a token) | **20-25 ms**, every draw | each canvas polygon, circle or AA polyline costs 17-33 µs in Godot 4 (a GPU buffer per polygon; benchmarked), ~15 a token, 30 tokens |
| tokens' text, names, late marks | ~8-10 ms | |

It also draws **twice a frame** while a tween moves the view (the tween's `_apply_view` redraw is
flushed at the start of the next frame, then `_process` redraws "live" again). And it went on drawing
every frame **under the battlefield**, through the battle's camera, unseen: a third of a second a frame
as the camera tipped down and rose back, 7-15 ms a frame through the fight. The land's quality watchdog
read those slow frames as the land's fault and stepped it down to its lowest step (no shadows, no MSAA,
0.7 scale) by the second battle.

Smaller things, mine: the battle camera's search (`_battle_rig`) took 35-50 ms of the frame the
battlefield opened; the dice tray's four pieces were loaded from disk then (100 ms); the earthworks
models in the swoop back (128 ms); the field's sounds through the fight (1-10 ms each).

### What changed (my files)

- **The battlefield rests the board under it** (`land_field.gd` `_cover`): from the end of its fade in
  to the start of its fade out the board's overlay is hidden, so it isn't drawn through the battle's
  camera.
- **The battle camera's sight test** (`land_field.gd` `_sees_all`) asks only where the answer could be
  no: a point of a sight line above the highest ground and the highest roof hides nothing, and only a
  building whose walls come near the line can stand in it. Same shots (checked against the full test at
  every yaw and pitch on three maps, 168 searches, 0 differences; and every battle in v24p), 35-50 ms →
  3.5-6 ms.
- **A battle's loads come with the land** (`land.gd` `warm_list`): the figures' models (the dice tray's
  pieces among them), the dice, tin and tracer shaders, every sound effect, the charge music, all loaded
  in the land's background build and held; the earthworks models load with the scenery (`scenery.gd`).
- **Figures are reused** between battles (`diorama.gd` `_spare`), and the board's garrisons too.
- **The land's quality watchdog** (`land.gd` `_land_heavy`) steps down only when drawing the land is a
  good share of a slow frame (its measured GPU or render time). Compatibility (no timings) as before.

### What's left, and it isn't mine (map_board.gd is v24-map's)

The swoop itself (the board zooming in, before the battlefield opens) and the board on the land in
general are still the overlay's cost. Prototyped and measured here, not on this branch:

1. `_draw_region_labels` / `_bonus_spot`: search again only once the view has held still 0.15 s
   (debounce, not throttle; while it moves, keep the old spot scaled even when the box's size changed),
   and skip `_region_at` for a spot that can't win (`hit > 0 and cost >= own_cost`: same result). The
   patch is `v24-3d-map_board.patch` in the shared notes folder. With it, the swoop's worst frame fell
   from 277 ms to 60 ms in the same run.
2. The tokens' shapes, ~20-25 ms a draw on the land: caching the shape points (GDScript) didn't help; it
   needs fewer polygon calls a token (each token's shape stack drawn once into a cached texture and
   blitted). v24-map is changing the token's shield this round, so it's theirs.
3. The double draw in a tween: `_apply_view` could mark the overlay dirty for `_process` to redraw once.

For v24-battles (battle_theater.gd's): on the land the flat field's backdrop (`assets/battle/field_*.webp`,
2.5 MB each) is still loaded at every battle's setup though it's hidden (`_bg.texture = Style.tex(...)`
in `_setup_engagement`): 40-100 ms the first time each terrain is fought on.

## 2. Bullet paths (Alex: "a little too polygon-y and wide")

The musket ball's streak was a six-sided cone 2.7 wide (a sixth of a man's height). It's now a ribbon
that turns its face to the camera (`game/world3d/tracer.gdshader`): 0.9 wide at the ball, tapering, soft
at its edges and fading to nothing behind, in the firer's colour, never under a pixel and a half on
screen, and its tail never reaches back past the muzzle. One quad mesh and one material an army, not a
mesh and a material a shot. The cannon ball is round (24 by 12 for 10 by 6). Before and after:
`v24-3d-shots/tracers_before_after_1.png`, `_2.png`, and in the game `tracers_in_game.png` (shared notes
folder).

## 3. A fight at a fort (Alex: "have the defending army defending from within/outside the fort against the direction(s) of the foe(s)")

Done, staging only. Before, a battle at a fort land lifted the fort off the table and fought on the
open ground. Now (`land_field.gd` `_at_the_fort`):

- the fort stays, and is the fight's heart: the camera takes it in;
- each of the defender's groups lines the wall that faces its foe, two deep, its horse and guns beside
  it; its drummer and colours stand inside the walls on that side, and its commander in the middle;
- each attacking column halts on its road in, facing that wall, four men's heights off;
- when the defenders win a volley the fort's guns are seen to fire too: a flash and smoke off the
  bastion facing the foe (the sound was already there).

And the ground under every fort is levelled (`terrain.gd` `level`): seated at the lowest of its slope
(as all scenery is), a fort stood half buried. Redgate had 36 of fall under ramparts 10 high; Briargate
14. Cut into the hill to the height of its lower third, eased out over a third of its width, on its own
land and clear of the shore. That shows on the board too.
