# v2.4: v24-3d (the swoop, the bullet paths, a fight at a fort)

Branch `claude/v24-3d-93ee66`, from 017a215 (v2.3.1), with v24-map's branch merged in (8ed8ba6) for the
token cache. No rules change, no outcome change: drawing, staging and loading only.

## 1. The chug between the map and a battle (Alex: "the zoom in between the main map and battle map on 3d absolutely chugs")

### What it was (measured, not guessed)

`tools/study/swoop.gd` plays the real client on the land as Alex plays it (board on the land, battles on
the land, motion "all", rolling himself, Wide World) through several battles. It records every frame's
real length (`Time.get_ticks_usec`, the first thing each frame does) and, for the slow ones, what ran in
them: resources newly loaded from disk, nodes and resources made, pipelines compiled (by kind), draw
calls, process time, render CPU and GPU time (the screen's and the land's), and the board's overlay
draws, timed.

On this PC (RTX 3070, i7-10700KF, Vulkan Forward+, 1440x900), **render and GPU were 1-6 ms a frame.**
It wasn't the 3D, and it wasn't asset loading. **It was the board's 2D overlay** (`map_board.gd`
`_draw_overlay`, drawn through the land's camera by `projector`/`unprojector`):

| piece | cost per overlay draw | why |
|---|---|---|
| `_draw_region_labels` → `_bonus_spot` | **20-415 ms** while the camera moves | `_bonus_at` was cleared every 0.15 s while the zoom or the layout changed, and each search tried up to 2,673 spots per region, asking `_region_at` (an `unproject` through the 3D camera, 6 height lookups) three times a spot |
| the tokens' shapes (`Style.draw_shape` ×5, `draw_circle` ×5 a token) | **20-25 ms**, every draw | each canvas polygon, circle or AA polyline costs 17-33 µs in Godot 4 (a GPU buffer made per polygon; benchmarked), ~15 a token on the land, 30 tokens |
| tokens' text, names, late marks | ~8-10 ms | |

It drew **twice a frame** while a tween moved the view (the tween's redraw was drawn at the next frame's
start through the land's camera of the frame before, then again once the land's camera caught up). And it
went on drawing every frame **under the battlefield**, through the battle's camera, unseen: a third of a
second a frame as the camera tipped down and rose back, 7-15 ms a frame through the fight. The land's
quality watchdog read those slow frames as the land's fault and stepped it down to its lowest step (no
shadows, no MSAA, 0.7 scale) by the second battle.

Smaller, in the 3D: the battle camera's search (`_battle_rig`) took 35-50 ms of the frame the battlefield
opened; the dice tray's four pieces were loaded from disk then (100 ms); the earthworks models in the swoop
back (128 ms); the field's sounds through the fight (1-10 ms each).

### What changed, step by step (each its own commit)

1. **The 3D side** (0274a76):
   - the battlefield rests the board under it (`land_field.gd` `_cover`): from the end of its fade in to
     the start of its fade out the board's overlay is hidden, so it isn't drawn through the battle's camera;
   - the battle camera's sight test (`_sees_all`) asks only where the answer could be no (a point of a
     sight line above the highest ground and the highest roof hides nothing; only a building whose walls
     come near the line can stand in it): the same shots (168 searches on three maps, 0 different; v24p
     checks every battle), 35-50 ms → 3.5-6 ms;
   - a battle's loads come with the land (`land.gd` `warm_list`): the figures' models (the dice tray's
     pieces among them), the dice, tin and tracer shaders, every sound effect, the charge music, loaded in
     the land's background build and held; the earthworks models with the scenery (`scenery.gd`);
   - figures are reused between battles (`diorama.gd` `_spare`), and the board's garrisons too;
   - the land's quality watchdog (`land.gd` `_land_heavy`) steps down only when drawing the land is a good
     share of a slow frame (its measured GPU or render time); the Compatibility renderer as before.
2. **The bonus labels** (e205b31, `map_board.gd` `_draw_region_labels`, `_bonus_spot`, by the
   coordinator's call): placed again once the view has held still 0.15 s (meanwhile the old spot is
   scaled, whatever its size), and a spot that can't win is never asked about its ground (same result).
3. **One draw a frame in the swoops** (260e7ad, `swoop_to`/`swoop_back`): the tween keeps the time and a
   driver node moves the camera first thing in the frame's process, before the land's camera follows the
   board and with every other call for a redraw; the tween's last step moves it itself, so the battle
   opens on the camera where it should be.
4. **v24-map's tokens merged** (8ed8ba6): their shields and arrivals tab, the token body unchanged. The
   "before" for the cache.
5. **The tokens drawn from meshes** (09d6906, `game/board/token_mesh.gd`, peeled out of map_board.gd): a
   token's body (its shadow on the land, its thickness, rim, face, edge, sheen and shine), its garrison
   shield (each look, and G cycles them) and the road home's amber ring are each one mesh, made once for a
   look and a size (to the pixel under 20 px of radius, every two above) and drawn where the token stands.
   Through two battles 9,375 tokens are drawn from 18 meshes. They look as they did: 4.0/255 apart on
   average over the pixels they cover, the differences in where an antialiased edge falls within a pixel
   (0.3% of pixels off by more than 24/255 two pixels across). The old drawing stays (`token_meshes`) for
   that comparison.

## 2. Bullet paths (Alex: "a little too polygon-y and wide")

The musket ball's streak was a six-sided cone 2.7 wide (a sixth of a man's height). It's now a ribbon
that turns its face to the camera (`game/world3d/tracer.gdshader`): 0.9 wide at the ball, tapering, soft
at its edges and fading to nothing behind, in the firer's colour, never under a pixel and a half on
screen, and its tail never reaches back past the muzzle. One quad mesh and one material an army, not a
mesh and a material a shot. The cannon ball is round (24 by 12 for 10 by 6). Before and after:
`v24-3d-shots/tracers_before_after_1.png`, `_2.png`, and in the game `tracers_in_game.png` (shared notes
folder).

## 3. A fight at a fort (Alex: "have the defending army defending from within/outside the fort against the direction(s) of the foe(s)")

Done, staging only. Before, a battle at a fort land lifted the fort off the table and fought on the
open ground. Now (`land_field.gd` `_at_the_fort`):

- the fort stays, and is the fight's heart: the camera takes it in;
- each of the defender's groups lines the wall that faces its foe, two deep, its horse and guns beside
  it; its drummer and colours stand inside the walls on that side, and its commander in the middle;
- each attacking column halts on its road in, facing that wall, four men's heights off;
- when the defenders win a volley the fort's guns are seen to fire too: a flash and smoke off the
  bastion facing the foe (the sound was already there).

And the ground under every fort is levelled (`terrain.gd` `level`): seated at the lowest of its slope
(as all scenery is), a fort stood half buried. Redgate had 36 of fall under ramparts 10 high; Briargate
14. Cut into the hill to the height of its lower third, eased out over a third of its width, on its own
land and clear of the shore. That shows on the board too. Shots: `v24-3d-shots/fort_*` (shared notes
folder).
